The Experience

Sunday in Ipanema changes you.

"They close the road. The whole city walks into the street. No cars — just music, caipirinhas, and two million people who remembered what life is for."

That's why this trip exists. Antonio went to Rio, couldn't stop talking about Sunday in Ipanema, and instead of answering the same question fifty times — he's taking a group.

Five days in Rio de Janeiro. Twelve people who move — or are building the thing that will. Antonio as your host. The JW Marriott as your base. Sunday on Ipanema as the centerpiece.

This isn't a tour. There's no itinerary you'll follow in a group of thirty. It's twelve people with real access, moving through one of the most alive cities on earth, with someone who's already mapped it.

Your Host

Antonio Dixon.

Antonio Dixon
MIT EMBA Oxford Forbes Hedge Fund AI / Fintech

Rio doesn't give up her secrets to people passing through. She gives them to people who stay.

Antonio lived there — on and off, four years. Not in a hotel with a view and a return flight already booked. In an apartment above a padaria in Copacabana, where the owner knew his order before he said it. He's gone back four times a year, every year, since — the way some men return to a war, or a city that never finished with them.

Say Manaus to him and he'll tell you about the Rio Negro running black beside the Amazon for miles, two rivers refusing to mix. Say Lençóis and he's not thinking about Rio at all — he's thinking of Lençóis Maranhenses, white dunes stacked to the horizon with turquoise rain lagoons sitting in the valleys between them, like the desert forgot it wasn't supposed to hold water. Say São Luís and he'll tell you about reggae coming out of a colonial square older than the United States — Brazilians call it "Jamaica," and they're not exaggerating. Recife, where the canals cut through downtown like a tropical Amsterdam nobody photographs correctly. Salvador, where the drums in Pelourinho don't stop at midnight — they just change rhythm, because Bahia is Africa that never left. Natal, dune buggies going sideways down sand at an angle that shouldn't be survivable. Pipa, cliffs dropping into water clear enough to watch the dolphins from the beach. Fortaleza. Búzios, where Brigitte Bardot showed up in the sixties, told the world, and the world showed up too — and somehow it's still exactly what it was before her. And Florianópolis at the other end of it, where the fishermen still haul their nets by hand.

Twenty-one cities and counting. He didn't fly to most of them — a lot of it was overland, bus and boat and whatever a stranger would sell him a seat in that week. You don't understand this country from thirty thousand feet. You understand it from the window of a bus you weren't sure was going to leave.

MIT-educated, Oxford-trained, Forbes-recognized — that's the résumé. Four years lived in Rio, twenty-one cities from Manaus to Florianópolis, and a country learned overland instead of from a screen — that's the reason he's the one taking you.

The trip is designed the way Antonio moves: direct access, no tourist packaging, real immersion. You'll see what matters — and you'll see it with someone who already paid for the map in time, not money.

Questions

Everything you're wondering.

Who is this trip for? +
People who are building something, running something, or thinking about both. Founders, investors, people in finance, tech, and the spaces between. You don't need a title — you need to be the kind of person who moves.
What's the actual vibe? +
Intimate and unforced. Twelve people is a dinner party, not a conference. Every day is planned — boat rides, churrasco nights, sunrise hikes — so you're never standing around figuring out what to do next. Antonio hosts and curates the whole thing. Think: real people, real conversations, a city that earns your full attention.
Is this safe? Rio has a reputation. +
Rio has neighborhoods and neighborhoods. The Zona Sul (South Zone) — Ipanema, Leblon, Copacabana — where the JW Marriott sits is very safe, walkable, and absolutely alive. We stay in the right part of the city and we move with awareness, not anxiety. Antonio has done the research. The vibe is Rio for grown-ups.
What flights should I book? +
Fly into Rio de Janeiro — Galeão International Airport (GIG). Arrive by evening on October 22. Depart anytime October 27. Once you've paid your deposit, message Antonio on Instagram and he'll share the flight timing that works best for the group pickup.
Do I need to speak Portuguese? +
No. The JW Marriott operates in English, guided experiences are bilingual, and Antonio knows enough Portuguese to navigate. The city will also teach you five words in the first hour.
I'm coming alone — will I know anyone? +
Most people are. That's the point. Twelve people in a new city is one of the fastest ways to build genuine relationships. By night two, you'll know everyone. By Sunday in Ipanema, you'll have people you'll call in five years.
Can I do double occupancy with someone I'm not travelling with? +
Yes. If you don't have a room partner, note it in your DM to Antonio. He'll match same-gender room shares for the double rate. No awkwardness — people have done this before.
What's the refund policy? +
Deposits are non-refundable but transferable. If you can't make it, you can send someone else in your place — same terms, same price. After September 1, full balances are non-refundable and non-transferable. Trip is locked at that point.
What currency do I need? +
Brazilian Real (BRL). ATMs are available at GIG airport and throughout Ipanema. USD is occasionally accepted but at poor rates. Bring a card with no foreign transaction fees. The JW accepts all major cards.
